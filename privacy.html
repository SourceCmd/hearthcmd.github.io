Privacy Policy

Effective: [EFFECTIVE DATE]

Hearth Cmd is an app for households and their AI agents, made by Verge Labs LLC ("we", "us"). This policy explains what information is collected when you use Hearth Cmd, why, how long it's kept, and the choices you have. It covers the Hearth Cmd app, the relay that connects it (at relaycmd.com), and this website.

1. How Hearth Cmd is designed

Hearth Cmd is built so that your data lives on your machine, not on our servers:

  • Your keep holds your household. A keep is the Keep Cmd software running on a computer you own. It holds your household's data in a database on that computer: its members, agents, the agents' conversations and memory, rules and approvals, screens, and the credentials for any accounts you connect. We don't operate your keep, and it is designed so that we can't read what it holds.
  • The relay delivers. When your phone and your keep aren't on the same network, they reach each other through our relay. Traffic between your app and your keep is end-to-end encrypted by design, so the relay carries data it isn't able to read. On your home network, the app may connect to your keep directly.
  • Household chat uses Loop Cmd's encrypted conversations. Chat rooms in Hearth Cmd are end-to-end encrypted group conversations on the same system as our Loop Cmd app. Your keep is a member of each of your household's rooms, so that it can keep each room's members in step with the household's. This means your keep, and so whoever controls the computer it runs on, can read household chat.
  • Agents talk to AI model providers. Your agents run on your keep and use the AI model providers you choose (such as Anthropic or OpenAI), under your own accounts with them. What an agent works on, including messages people send it, is sent to that provider. See Third parties.

These describe how the service is designed. No system is perfectly secure, and we can't promise that it will always work as intended.

2. What we collect

Our relay holds what it needs to sign you in, deliver data and limit abuse:

  • Email address. Used to sign you in with a one-time code, to deliver invitations you send or receive, and to contact you about your account. When someone invites an address to a household, the relay keeps that address and which keep it's for until the invitation is accepted, withdrawn or expires.
  • Account and device identifiers. Random identifiers for your account and for each app install you sign in from, the public keys your devices use, which app each install is, and when an account or device was last active.
  • Push notification tokens. A token from Apple for each device, used to deliver notifications. Notification contents are generally encrypted on the sending side so that only your device can show them.
  • Your keep's registration. That you run a keep, its public key, its software version, and when it was last seen. If you turn on health reports, your keep also sends content-free diagnostics (such as version, uptime and error counts by type).
  • Who may reach which keep. Your keep tells the relay which accounts may connect to it, so the relay can route members' apps to it. The relay isn't told your household's name or its members' names.
  • Encrypted data held for delivery. When a device or your keep is offline, the relay holds encrypted data until it can be delivered: messages between apps and keeps for up to about 7 days, and household chat messages and attachments for up to about 30 days. The relay can see the size of each item, when it was sent, which account sent it, and who belongs to each conversation, but it is designed not to be able to read the contents.
  • Delivery traces. Identifiers, outcomes, sizes and timings of deliveries, kept for 14 days so we can answer "why didn't this arrive?".
  • Abuse controls. To limit spam, the relay caps how many first contacts an account can make in a day, counted from the message requests it holds.
  • Logs. Our servers keep operational logs for a limited period. They are designed to hold identifiers, types, timings and error codes, not the contents of your messages.

3. What stays on your devices and your keep

  • Display names, household and room names, and the contents of messages and files are kept on your devices and your keep, and travel through the relay only in encrypted form.
  • Agents' work: their conversations, memory, files and the actions they ask to take are on your keep.
  • Connected accounts (for example, a calendar): your keep holds the tokens and talks to the provider itself. Our servers may pass along a code while you connect an account, which is designed to be useless without a secret held on your keep.
  • Voice and screens. A Home Assistant voice device or a wall display you pair talks to your keep over your own network. We don't receive audio. The text of what's said to an agent becomes part of that agent's conversation, which goes to its model provider.

Because this data is on hardware you control, keeping it safe, backed up or deleted is generally up to you. If you lose your devices and your keep's recovery key, we may not be able to help you recover it.

4. Third parties

We don't sell your information, and the app is designed without advertising, third-party analytics or tracking. Information reaches others in these ways:

  • AI model providers you choose. Your agents send their work to the providers you set up, under your own accounts and their terms. Whether a provider keeps or trains on that content depends on your arrangement with them, not on us.
  • Service providers who help us run the relay: currently Akamai (Linode) for servers and file storage, Brevo for sending email (your address and sign-in codes), and Apple for push notifications (push tokens and encrypted notification contents).
  • Services you connect. If you connect an account such as a calendar, your keep exchanges data with that service directly, under your account with it.
  • When required. We may disclose information we hold if we believe the law requires it, or to protect people's safety or the service. What we hold is limited to what this policy describes.

5. How long things are kept

  • Your email address, identifiers and keep registration: while your account exists.
  • Invitations: until accepted, withdrawn or expired.
  • Encrypted data waiting for delivery: up to about 7 days (between apps and keeps) or about 30 days (household chat and attachments).
  • Delivery traces: 14 days.
  • Logs: for a limited period.
  • Backups of the relay's database: for a limited period (currently up to about 90 days).
  • Your keep's data: on your computer, for as long as you keep it there.

6. Deleting your account

You can ask us to delete your account by emailing support@vergelabs.org from the address you sign in with, and in the app where available. Deleting your account removes it from the relay: your email address, devices and push tokens, and any data waiting to be delivered to you.

Your keep isn't deleted with your account. It and its data stay on your computer until you remove them there. Messages you sent remain on the devices of the people who received them. Deleted data may remain in our backups for a limited period (currently up to about 90 days) before it's gone.

7. Children

Hearth Cmd is not intended for anyone under 13, or under the minimum age where they live if that is higher. We don't knowingly collect information from children under that age. If you believe a child has signed up, contact us and we'll take steps to remove their account.

8. Your choices and rights

You can delete your account at any time (see above). Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct or delete information we hold about you, or to object to how it's used. To ask, contact us at the address below. Much of your information is on your own devices and keep, where you can view and remove it yourself.

9. This website
