Privacy Policy

Effective: November 26, 2026

Hearth Cmd is an app for households and their AI agents, made by Verge Labs LLC ("we", "us"). This policy explains what information is collected when you use Hearth Cmd, why, how long it's kept, and the choices you have. It covers the Hearth Cmd app for iPhone and Android, the relay that connects it (at relaycmd.com), and this website.

Using the earlier app, now called Hearth Cmd Classic? It works differently, and has its own privacy policy.

1. How Hearth Cmd is designed

Hearth Cmd is built so that your household's data lives on your machine, not on our servers:

  • Your Keep holds your household. A Keep is the Keep Cmd software running on a computer you own. It holds your household's data on that computer: its members and their roles, invitations, rooms, agents and their conversations, rules and approvals, displays and voice devices, notes you write for your agents, and the credentials for any accounts you connect. We don't operate your Keep, and it's designed so that we can't read what it holds.
  • End-to-end encryption, households included. What travels between your app and your Keep is encrypted end to end, and so are your household's chat rooms. Our relay delivers this data and is designed so that it can't read it. It stores only encrypted data and the routing information described below. We hold no copy of your keys.
  • Household rooms. Chat rooms in Hearth Cmd are end-to-end encrypted group conversations on the same system as our Loop Cmd app. Your Keep is a member of each of your household's rooms, so that it can keep each room's members in step with the household's. This means your Keep, and so whoever controls the computer it runs on, can read household chat.
  • Agents talk to AI model providers. Your agents run on your Keep and use the AI model providers its owner chooses (such as Anthropic or OpenAI), under the owner's own accounts with them. What an agent works on, including messages household members send it, is sent to that provider. See Third parties.
  • Your data isn't in your phone's backups. Hearth Cmd's data on your phone isn't included in iCloud Backup, Android's backup or device-to-device transfer, or a computer backup. A new phone connects to your Keep again. On iPhone, your account's identity key is kept in iCloud Keychain, which Apple end-to-end encrypts, so a new iPhone or iPad on the same Apple ID can sign in as you; it holds no messages.

Two ways to connect

  • On your home network only, with no account. You can pair Hearth Cmd with your Keep by scanning the code it shows, or opening its pairing link, on your own network. You don't need an account with us, and the app talks to your Keep directly, not through our relay. The features that need the relay (using Hearth Cmd away from home, notifications, household rooms and inviting people) say so where they appear, and you can turn on Away from Home later in Account.
  • With an account. Sign in with your email address or phone number to use Hearth Cmd away from home and to join or invite others to a household. Your app then reaches your Keep through our relay, and, when you're on the same network, it may connect to your Keep directly. If Loop Cmd is already signed in on the same phone, Hearth Cmd can offer to continue as that account with one tap; it reads that from Loop Cmd on your phone, and nothing is sent until you tap.

These describe how the service is designed. No system is perfectly secure, and we can't promise that it will always work as intended.

2. What we collect

If you use Hearth Cmd on your home network only, the app doesn't send us information about you. If you sign in, the relay can't read your household's data, but it does need some information to sign you in, deliver data and limit abuse:

  • Email address and phone number. Your account has an email address, a US phone number, or both. They're used to sign you in with a one-time code and to contact you about your account. Hearth Cmd's emails come from hearth@sourcecmd.com, and its text messages carry only codes. Your account is your Source Cmd account, the same one Loop Cmd uses.
  • Household invitations. We don't send invitations by email or text: when someone in your household invites you, they let you know themselves. When a household invites an email address or phone number, the relay keeps that address or number and which Keep it's for until the invitation is answered or expires. The relay isn't told the household's name or its members' names; the invitation's details come to your app from the Keep, encrypted. To limit invitations to an hourly allowance, the relay counts how many invitations a Keep files, by the minute, and keeps the counts for an hour.
  • Who may reach which Keep. A Keep tells the relay which accounts may connect to it (its household's members), so the relay can route their apps to it and deliver its notifications to them.
  • Account and device identifiers. Random identifiers for your account and each app install, which of our apps and which version each install runs, and the day they were last active.
  • Passkeys. If you add a passkey, the relay keeps its public key so you can sign in with it, until you remove it.
  • One-tap sign-in between our apps. When Hearth Cmd or Loop Cmd is signed in, the relay keeps the public half of a key that lets the other app on the same phone sign in with one tap, and a record that it did, so each key can sign in the other app only once.
  • Device checks. On iPhone, the app uses Apple's App Attest and DeviceCheck so the relay can check it's talking to a genuine install of Hearth Cmd, typically when you sign in, invite someone or add a device (not on each message). DeviceCheck may also be used to keep a ban in place if the app is reinstalled on the same phone. Hearth Cmd for Android doesn't use a device check yet.
  • Starting over. If you start over without your devices, the relay keeps a record of the request for 30 days, to limit how often an account can start over.
  • Your public keys and device list. Your public identity key and a list, signed by you, of your devices and agents. The relay publishes it so others can encrypt to you. This means the relay knows how many devices and agents you have.
  • Push notification tokens. A token for each device, from Apple on iPhone or from Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging on Android, used to deliver notifications. Notification contents, such as an agent's request or a room's new message, are encrypted on the sending side so only your device can show them.
  • Who is in which room. The relay knows the members of each household room and since when, because it needs this to deliver messages, and keeps each room's public group information (its members, their public keys and its admins), never its messages or its name.
  • Message metadata. For each encrypted message it holds, which account sent it, to which room or Keep, when, and its size. Sizes are padded so they reveal less, but timing isn't hidden.
  • Reports and blocks. If you report someone in a room, recent messages you review (typically the last ten or so) are sent to us readable, but sealed so that only an offline key we hold can open them. The relay also records who reported whom. We keep reports for up to about 90 days. If you block someone, the relay records it so it can enforce it.
  • Attachments. Files you send in a room are encrypted on your device and stored in our file storage for up to about 90 days, whether or not they've been delivered, so the room's members can fetch them. The relay knows their size (padded to a less revealing size), which room they belong to, and who uploaded them. Files can be up to 100 MB each.
  • If you run a Keep. The relay records your Keep (an identifier, its public key, its software version and operating system, when it was last seen, and, if you turn health reports on, a daily report with no content, such as version, uptime and error counts by type), and holds encrypted messages for your Keep while it's offline, such as an answer you give from a notification, for up to about 7 days.
  • Logs. Our servers keep operational logs for a limited period. They're designed to hold identifiers, types, timings and error codes, not the contents of your messages, and our web server's logs are designed to leave out IP addresses.

Names of people, households, rooms and agents, profile details, and the contents of rooms and of your agents' work stay on your devices, your household members' devices and your Keep. They reach the relay only in encrypted form.

3. What stays on your devices and your Keep

  • Your household's roster: who is in the household, what they're called there, their roles, and the devices they use, kept on your Keep.
  • Agents' work: their conversations, memory, files and the actions they ask to take, and the rules and approvals that answer them, are on your Keep. Notes you write about yourself or your household for your agents are on your Keep and given to the agents, which may send them to their model provider.
  • Connected accounts (for example, a calendar): your Keep holds the tokens and talks to the service itself. Our servers don't receive the tokens; at most, a sign-in code may pass through on its way back to your Keep, which is designed to be useless without a secret held there.
  • Displays and voice devices. A wall display or a voice device (such as a Home Assistant voice satellite) that you pair talks to your Keep over your own network, not through our relay. We don't receive audio. The text of what's said to an agent becomes part of that agent's conversation, which goes to its model provider.

Because this data is on hardware you control, keeping it safe, backed up or deleted is generally up to you, and whoever runs the Keep can see what it holds. If you lose your devices and your Keep, we may not be able to help you recover your data.

Reporting and blocking

You can report or block a person from their details in a household room. Before a report is sent, you see the recent messages that will go with it, and Block is offered alongside. Reports are designed so that action on an account generally needs reports from several independent, established accounts; we may then limit or suspend it. Reporting someone's agent reports its owner. You can also report abuse by email (see Contact).

4. Voice in the app

  • Dictation on iPhone. If you dictate a message, speech is turned into text on your device. The first time, the app downloads its speech model from Hugging Face, which sees your device's IP address as with any download; no audio or text is sent.
  • Dictation on Android. Dictation uses your phone's speech recognition service. Where your phone can recognize speech on the device, Hearth Cmd uses that, and audio stays on the phone. Otherwise it uses the phone's default speech recognition service, which may process audio as your phone's maker or that service's provider decides, including on their servers. Audio never reaches us.
  • Spoken replies. If you turn on spoken replies, your phone's own text-to-speech reads them aloud. On Android, that's the speech engine your phone is set to use.
  • The text you dictate is sent to the agent like anything you type.

5. Third parties

We don't sell your information, and the app is designed without advertising, third-party analytics or tracking. Information reaches others in these ways:

  • Your household. The members of a room see what's sent in it. The Keep's owner, and whoever controls the computer it runs on, can see what the Keep holds.
  • AI model providers the Keep's owner chooses. Agents send their work to the providers set up on the Keep, under the owner's own accounts and those providers' terms. Whether a provider keeps or trains on that content depends on that arrangement, not on us.
  • Services you connect. If you connect an account such as a calendar, your Keep exchanges data with that service directly, under your account with it.
  • Service providers who help us run the relay: currently Akamai (Linode) for servers and file storage, Brevo for sending email and text messages (your address or number, and the codes), Apple for push notifications on iPhone (push tokens and encrypted notification contents) and for device checks (App Attest and DeviceCheck), and Google for push notifications on Android, through Firebase Cloud Messaging (push tokens and encrypted notification contents).
  • Voice services, as described in Voice in the app: Hugging Face for the iPhone speech model's download, and on Android, your phone's speech recognition service.
  • When required. We may disclose information we hold if we believe the law requires it, or to protect people's safety or the service. What we hold is limited to what this policy describes.

All the above categories exclude text messaging originator opt-in data and consent; this information won’t be shared with any third parties.

6. How long things are kept

  • Your email address or phone number, identifiers, keys and device list: while your account exists.
  • Each device: while it's signed in. Passkeys: until you remove them. One-tap sign-in keys: while the device that registered one is signed in.
  • Household invitations: until answered or expired. A Keep's invitation counts: an hour.
  • Who may reach a Keep: while the household admits them.
  • Room membership: while the room has members.
  • Encrypted room messages waiting for delivery: until delivered, up to about 90 days. Attachments: up to about 90 days. Messages waiting for an offline Keep: up to about 7 days.
  • Sign-in codes: typically removed a day after they expire. Sign-in sessions: removed when they expire. Start-over requests: 30 days.
  • Who you've blocked: while both accounts exist.
  • Reports and abuse counts: up to about 90 days, including, in the limited form described below, after the account they concern is deleted.
  • Logs: for a limited period, generally up to about 7 days for our web server and about 14 days for system logs.
  • Backups of the relay's database: generally up to about 30 days.
  • Your Keep's data: on your computer, for as long as it's kept there.

7. Deleting your account

Your account is your Source Cmd account, used by Hearth Cmd and Loop Cmd. In Hearth Cmd, open Account → Delete account (shown when you're signed in). You'll be asked to confirm with a code sent to your email address or phone number. If you use Hearth Cmd on your home network only, you have no account with us to delete.

Without the app, email support@vergelabs.org from your account's email address and ask us to delete it; if your account has only a phone number, include the number. We confirm it's you by sending a code to the account's email address or phone number, then delete the account within 30 days and tell you when it's done. See Delete your account.

Deleting your account:

  • removes your account from the relay: your email address and phone number, your passkeys, your devices and push tokens, your public keys and device list, the relay's record of any Keep you run, and any messages and files waiting for you;
  • takes you out of every room and conversation (the people in them see you leave), and removes you from the relay's list of who may reach each household's Keep;
  • signs out the device you used.

Your Keep isn't deleted with your account. It and its data stay on your computer until you remove them there (run keepcmd uninstall). A household you belong to is kept on its owner's Keep; you can leave it from the Household tab, and a household's owner can delete it there, which removes its data from the Keep. Messages you sent remain on the devices of the people who received them. Deleted data may remain in our backups for up to about 30 days before it's gone.

To prevent abuse, a little is kept after you delete your account. Reports other people made about your account, and, where something counts against it, its abuse counts, are generally kept for up to about 90 days from when they were made, filed under a one-way code derived from your email address or phone number rather than under your account, so that deleting an account can't be used to clear them. If the same email address or phone number is used to sign up again in that time, they may be linked to the new account. Reports you made are kept for their remaining time without saying who made them. If your account was suspended for abuse when you deleted it, the devices it used may remain unable to sign up again. An account with no reports against it leaves none of this behind.

8. Children

Hearth Cmd is not intended for anyone under 13, or under the minimum age where they live if that is higher. We don't knowingly collect information from children under that age. If you believe a child has signed up, contact us and we'll take steps to remove their account.

9. Your choices and rights

You can use Hearth Cmd without an account, on your home network, and you can delete your account at any time (see above). Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct or delete information we hold about you, or to object to how it's used. To ask, contact us at the address below. Much of your information is on your own devices and Keep, where you can view and remove it yourself.
